# No one sits / physical pile revision 05
A seamless white stage with floor only. 240 desktop / 96 mobile chairs, 24 geometry families, individual scales, continuously varied wood colours and upholstery. Each chair is dropped into a compact pile using gravity and dynamic compound colliders. Actual body positions and neighbourhood density drive selection and cooperative construction fronts. Compatible pairs can transfer physical parts, which retain their IDs, scales and finishes; active parts are force-driven, then regrouped at their actual solved poses. Nearby rebuilt assemblies may form temporary fixed joints; all remain dynamic. No geometry teleport on docking. Rendering uses locally generated softbox environment, two shadow sources on desktop, GTAO and optional wood PBR maps. This is approximate real-time rendering and not a load-bearing engineering simulation. The count is reduced to keep full rigid-body collision costs manageable. Actual pile density, joint/contact behaviour, FPS and appearance require browser testing. Three.js MIT; Rapier Apache-2.0.
Live collision events now maintain a contact graph for selection and physical joint creation; failed assemblies retry without forced large-gap welds. Component finish travels with the rigid part. Colour uploads only occur after actual exchanges or reset.

## Floating collective / revision 09
Zero gravity throughout. All compound chairs participate in a continuously sampled spatial-neighbourhood field with density, velocity alignment, contact signals, four changing flow regimes, and soft confinement. Persistent contacts can create temporary physical bonds. Reconstruction departs tangentially with inherited momentum, staggered disassembly and force-driven docking. No position keyframes. Flow regimes are procedural, not a learned neural model; construction still uses adaptive heuristic policies and compatible parts. No browser physics or visual validation has been completed for this revision.

Revision 10: local pressure increases with neighbourhood density, with a wider repulsive radius and weaker long-range compression. Point-field proxy tests cover 60 seconds with both populations; these are not rigid-body or GPU tests.
